**Caption:**
Day 5 — the original smoked tea. 🔥🌲

Lapsang Souchong (正山小种) is the world's first black tea, born in the Wuyi Mountains over 400 years ago. Legend says that during the Ming Dynasty, a passing army delayed the tea harvest, and the frantic tea makers rushed to dry the leaves over pinewood fires to save them — creating an accidental masterpiece.

Our traditional Lapsang Souchong is made exactly this way. The leaves are withered, rolled, and oxidised, then slowly dried over smouldering pinewood — not chemical "liquid smoke" or artificial flavouring. The result is a tea of astonishing complexity: the smoke is present but refined, like a distant campfire in a pine forest, layered with dried longan, dark chocolate, and a subtle sweetness that lingers.

This is a tea that demands attention. It pairs beautifully with dark chocolate, aged cheese, or simply a quiet evening by the fire.

Experience the original. Available now. 🌲

**Caption:**
Day 6 — let's brew. 🫖

Wuyi rock oolongs reveal their true character through gongfu-style brewing. Here's the simple method we recommend:

**What you'll need:**
- 5g of tea (roughly 1 tbsp of dry leaves)
- A gaiwan or small teapot (~100ml)
- Water at 95°C (203°F) — just off the boil

**The method:**
1️⃣ Rinse — Pour hot water over the leaves and immediately discard. This "awakens" the leaves and removes any dust. Breathe in the aroma of the wet leaves — this is called the "soul of the tea."
2️⃣ Steep — Pour fresh hot water and steep for 10-15 seconds for the first infusion.
3️⃣ Increase — Add 5-10 seconds for each subsequent infusion. Quality rock oolongs will give 6-10+ infusions, each revealing a new layer.
4️⃣ Enjoy — Sip slowly. Let the tea coat your entire mouth. Notice the "yan yun" — the mineral sweetness that lingers after swallowing.

Rock teas are incredibly forgiving. Don't overthink it — the best brewing method is the one you enjoy.
